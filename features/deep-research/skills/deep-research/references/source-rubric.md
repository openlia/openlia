# Source Rubric

Use 0-5 scores and explain unusual scores in `notes`. A score represents the
source's usefulness for the specific research question, not whether the source
is universally trustworthy.

- **Authority:** closeness to primary evidence or the accountable institution.
- **Quality:** method clarity, specificity, reproducibility, and transparency.
- **Recency:** fit between the source date and the question's freshness need.
- **Relevance:** direct usefulness for the exact claim being tested.

The helper weights these dimensions as authority 0.4, quality 0.3, recency 0.1,
and relevance 0.2. Missing dimensions are treated as zero and reported as an
evidence gap. The weighted score is a source-usefulness triage aid, not a truth
score or an automatic recommendation.

Prefer primary sources, official records, transparent methods, and sources that
state dates and limitations. Use secondary sources to add context or challenge
the primary record, not to conceal missing primary evidence.
